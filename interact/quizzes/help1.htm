HELP FOR TEST 1

Q1: How many people on the ark?  Genesis states that Noah & his wife + his 3 sons and their wives were the only people to enter the ark. This means of course, that the entire world population is related to Noah and his wife.

Q2: How long were they in the ark?  The Bible details the exact day of the beginning and end of the ark voyage. Calculation of the allotted time is quite easy, but depends on the length of the month used. Some ancient calendars used a 30 day (lunar) month with periodic adjustments to the keep seasons aligned. This could alter the ark voyage by a few days, but approximately 371 - 376 days. 

Gen 7:11, in Noah's 600th year, the flood began on the 17th day of the 2nd month. In Gen 8:13, in Noah's 601st year, they were ready to leave the ark on the 27th day of the 2nd month. Using a 30 day month (or a 360 day year), this would be 371 days (including both the start and end days). By our 365 day year it would be 376 days. Possibly somewhere in between depending on how Noah's calendar worked.  

Q3: Where did the water come from? Genesis indicates 40 days of continuous rain. This is obviously not sufficient to cover the mountains, or even raise the sea level by more than a few inches. The atmosphere cannot hold enough water to do this. The Bible clearly states that water also came out of the ground through cracks (literally cleaved open). Such geological activities are insignificant today, but this description hold the key for understanding the mechanics of the flood. However it occurred, the floodwaters were almost certainly due to geological activities. One scientific model for mechanism of the flood is Runaway Subduction. This involves the sinking of the earth's crust into the softer (and lighter) mantle. Once this process begins, the increasing heat generated by the movement softens the mantle even more - accelerating the whole process. What results is a  planetary scale event, involving motion of tectonic plates supporting whole continents. Engineering based computer simulations have indicated continental drift accelerating to speeds of meters per second - easily explaining a rising sea level (or a tilting/sinking continent). Other mechanism have been suggested, though without the same degree of modeling and study as the Subduction model. 

Q4: How long did it take to build the ark? Although not as clearly stated as the ark voyage, the construction period could be taken as the time from God's warning. (Gen) which gives a period of 120 years. Alternatively, Noah is 500 years old when he had his (first) child Gen 5:32, followed by the account of the ark (Gen 6), which could imply a period of around 100 years. In either case, the construction time is sufficient. 

Q5: How was the door closed? "God shut him in" (Gen 7:16)

Q6: How old was Noah when the flood began? In Noah's 600th year. (Gen 7:11)

Q7: How many animals? This is an important question. An understanding of what animals were necessary to produce the variety we see today PLUS the animals that have become extinct since Noah. Lions and tigers can interbreed (ligers, tigons etc), so they can be viewed as a pedigree type of big cat. No need to keep 2 lions and 2 tigers then - but 1 liger + 1 tigon. The same applies to dog family and many other families. Some commentaries put the animal kinds as roughly equivalent to family rather than species. In "Noah's Ark - A Feasibility Study" Woodmorappe calculates around 14000 animals.

Q8: What was the ark made of?  Gopher wood is a classic mystery. The word is unknown, but it is fairly close to either PITCH or WAFER. It could also mean a species of wood, or possibly a treatment process. The translation "Cypress" is tenuous. 

Q9: How long was the ark? 300 cubits. The conversion to modern units depends on the definition of the cubit - the length from elbow to fingertip. There are a number of cubits used by the ancients. The most commonly cited cubit for the ark is the 18" cubit - corresponding to a person approximately 5ft 5in tall. One reason this cubit is chosen is to show that the ark had adequate capacity even using the smallest possible cubit. However, this is not necessarily the correct choice for several reasons. It is unlikely people were that short before the flood. A standard cubit likely originated from royalty or important people, perhaps Noah himself or an important ancestor such as Methuselah of even Adam. Some people claim the ark must have been measured in Egyptian cubits (the royal cubit being 20.6", the short being 17.6"). Their reasoning being that Moses was educated Egyptian royalty. While the royal Egyptian cubit is probably a likely candidate, it does not follow that Moses converted the original cubits into the nearest Egyptian equivalent. (For example: The dimensions 300 x 50 x 30 are round numbers, and indicate that no conversions have been carried out by Moses - more likely these are the exact numbers Noah used (and passed the information on to Shem, then Isaac etc). There are not many generations between Noah and Moses.

A likely scenario would be like this. Adam & Eve had lots of children. Within a few hundred years there were thousands. Cain's line was ahead, and developed technology for metalwork and musical instruments well ahead of Noah. By the time Noah was noteworthy, the world was quite possibly in the billions and very capable technologically. The scale and demanding requirements of the ark implies a fairly high level of technical prowess - fundamental to this is a standard unit of measure - a standard cubit. So it is most likely the cubit was standardized before or during Noah's lifetime - since high levels of trade demands it. So who had the famous forearm? Probably Adam, or maybe someone in Cain's line (they may have been ruling the trade, since they developed metalworking etc). Alternatively, Noah may have understood God's instructions as referring to his own arm.         

As for the Egyptian cubit, if it comes from some Pharaoh then it is completely irrelevant. There is also a Babylonian cubit of around 24", which is long, but not completely out of the question either. See cubits

Q10: Where did Noah live before the flood? The world before the flood was certainly different to our current knowledge. Geological changes were so dramatic that even coastlines and mountains were likely to have been completely rearranged. This makes the location of the Garden of Eden rather difficult, as well as any of the pre-flood landmarks.
