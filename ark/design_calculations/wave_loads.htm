PRIMARY HULL BENDING MODES

1. HOGGING

Cresting a wave. The top is in tension, the keel in compression. Side walls are in shear. The roof must be strong in tension here. The very thick keel should handle the compressive loads without buckling.

 

2. SAGGING 

Bridging a trough. Exactly the opposite of hogging. This time the top is in compression, the keel in tension, and  walls are in the opposite shear. The danger here is compression bucking of the roof. The roof will need a certain thickness to avoid bucking between frames.

 

3. RACKING 

Waves at an angle to the Ark. Top, keel and sides are in shear. The monocoque design maximizes torsional capacity, but the lengthwise slit of the roof window forms a weak spot. Each side of the roof needs to be tied together to transfer shear forces. See Integrated Roof

 

4. HORIZONTAL BENDING 

Uneven wave side load. Since the hull is wider than it is tall, and buoyancy (vertical) forces larger than side loads, this mode should not be a problem. Walls in tension/compression and top and bottom in shear.
