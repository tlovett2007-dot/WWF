Zooming in on Noah's Ark

An attempt at reverse engineering the Biblical Ark. (Talk)

ABSTRACT

A graphic rich presentation distilled from the author’s website www.worldwideflood.com and recent unpublished material on Noah's Ark.

The goal has been to highlight the potential range of Ark designs, explored through a combination of scriptural interpretation, historical clues and engineering predictions. The Bible gives important data while engineering can make certain estimations, but is it possible to fill in the details with any confidence?

Working on the foundation laid from the The Genesis Flood (1961) through to the Korean safety investigation published in 1993, a diverse group is attempting a crucial analysis of this ancient engineering wonder - Noah's Ark.

Included in the talk is a demolition of engineering allegations against an historical Ark, such as; structural impossibility of the wooden vessel; the need for iron bracing; the limitations of the size of wooden hulls, destruction by lethal waves; inadequacy of primitive technology. Many details are common sense, but it's not plain sailing as we navigate our way through obscure terms like “gopher wood”. 

The Bible gives a tantalizingly brief description of the Ark, but..."It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter." Proverbs 25:2

So we searching out the matter!

 

BACKGROUND 

Tim Lovett BE Mech, Dip Ed
Tim completed a bachelors degree in Mechanical Engineering at Sydney University in 1988, collecting a prize for mechanical design which he attributes to the unfair advantage of having spent his entire childhood making things. He worked for a number of years on the design of diagnostic ultrasound equipment and related ventures in experimental therapeutic use of high power ultrasound. Since completing a graduate diploma of Technical Education in 1993, Tim has been a full time instructor for 12 years in technical college engineering courses, while consulting in mechanical design, prototype development and low volume production technologies. More recently he was technical adviser for degree courses in Industrial Design and Engineering, developing equipment and resources for Rapid Prototyping, before dropping all extra-curricula activities to concentrate on Noah's Ark. Tim and Helen have six home schooled children.

MY STORY

I grew up in a Christian household, and never believed organic evolution because I was into making things and got to know Murphy's law like the back of my hand. And the back of my hand was a lesson in engineering design which I knew could not be an accident. I must confess in early high school I came up with my own explanation of history, which I later found the term for -  the day-age theory. I did Mechanical Engineering and was working in design of medical equipment. Having discovered the reality of God's grace on Sunday night Oct 7 1988, it became blindingly obvious by lunchtime the next day that people with an education were quick to bring in evolution and philosophy to avoid the gospel. We talk about defending the gospel, but this was about defending themselves from the gospel. A few years later, I heard an AiG speaker for the first time at my church, and jumped at the resources. I was was naturally drawn to the engineering of Noah's Ark, especially with the allegations against it by skeptics such as Plimer. Since then it has been a journey of discovery, the pace increasing all the while.
