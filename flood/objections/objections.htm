False Objections

 

Mount Everest covering flood

Including: 8000m sea level rise, rainfall calculations to obtain this rise,

The CPT model assumes today's mountains were mostly formed during the late stages of the flood. Since the modern oceans contain enough water to cover the earth to a depth of 3km, this is considered the terrain limit pre-flood.

 

Sea level raised by rainfall

Including: the atmosphere cannot hold this much water, the ark cannot sustain this level of rainfall

Rainfall was continuous, but in accord with CPT model it was more a side-effect of the tectonic activity and volcanism. Relative altitude changes of sea bed and continental landforms was predominately plate tectonics.

 

Nomadic Herdsmen can't build ocean liners

No indication of Noah being a cattle breeder.

 

Nonsense Objections

 

Altitude problems for the ark crew. 

A non-issue. No great altitude change is required in the flood model. Even if it did, this argument is incorrect - atmospheric pressure is due to the column of air above the mean sea level - which would remain virtually unaltered.

 

Genuine Objections

 

Sea too rough

If the geological damage was so severe, how could the ark survive?

 

The ark is too large for timber construction

If the ark was built from timber, it would leak badly or break apart in moderate seas.

 

Animals can't fit

Too many animals, and not enough space on the ark.

 

Noah could not attend to animal care

Feeding, cleaning and animal care requirements are too high for a crew of eight people.

 

Ancient shipbuilding not advanced enough

This idea reflects the thought that technological development has been increasing over the last 4-5 thousand years (with perhaps a few static periods). However, the maritime prowess indicated by the ark-sized Chinese junks and Greek triremes contradict this notion. The detailing of multi-layered planking in the enormous Greek triremes demonstrates their understanding of shear loads in the hull of a large vessel. Europeans never really got that far before steel arrived on the scene, although they did begin to supplement their hulls with steel bracing once this issue came to the fore ("biggest and fastest ship" contests of the late 1800's).     

So, a somewhat biased Eurocentric view gives misleading trends when attempting to extrapolate technology into dim dark history. World history reveals a number of spectacular examples of loss of technology - for example; the Chinese junks went the way of the bureaucrat. Government policy ended the Chinese super merchants, and China remained an inward focussed society until recently. The amazing sea-going junks were destroyed and even their plans burnt. Most are well aware that certain aspects of Egyptian, Greek and Roman technology were not regained for centuries - even millenia. (Construction, shipping, metalworking, working with granite, metrology, concrete, wartime equipment, roads, surveying, water service and city infrastructures, government, etc etc)

It is logical to expect a 500 year old Noah to have significant financial power, a very high intelligence by today's standards and a multi-skilled, multi-disciplined, 450 year working career. Given he had 100 to 120 years to build the vessel, there is little reason to doubt his ability to make the project happen, or to query his facilitation of the human and material resources necessary for the task.
