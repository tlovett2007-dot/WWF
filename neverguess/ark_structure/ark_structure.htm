TL: Mon 19 Apr 2004

Hi Jim;

Just got a reply back from Andrew at AiG. The ark shape issue looks like a goer;

>Andrew Lamb (information officer for AiG in Aust) just replied that the TJ editors think that doing a first article on SHAPE of the ark and its effects, Scripture etc. is a good idea. TJ author guidelines are at http://www.answersingenesis.org/Home/Area/Magazines/tj/tjguidelines.asp.

 

The TJ paper concept; Wow - looks like a serious paper...

1. Full or split article; I originally had plans to split it down into several parts, but it seems so much is integrated that it might be better to present the whole case in unity. However, it might get too big for a TJ article - maybe we should just focus on the blunt bow issue and the effects it has on the rest of the design. I guess the thing would be to see how well we could compress it anyway. Of course, the other option would be a summarized TJ article with a link to the backup calculations. (A bit like Hong should have been). But I think the round/flat issue has to be thoroughly addressed otherwise it might look like somebody just decided to do a study on a ship-like ark because they were used to that shape or something. It has to be quite a detailed focus on the hull shape issue in COMPARISON to the block shape.

2. Calcs. I'm just about to get my head into your tables - I managed to extract it from the Powerpoint file, but without the XL formulas. Could you attach the XL spreadsheet version - (Although I think I'll work it out). And I just had another thought too - ABS wave bending moment would obviously be based on average hull shapes - a flat bottomed, square ended hull would obviously have increased wave loading.

3. Roll, pitch buoyancy. If static roll and pitch buoyancy calculations are of any value I can't see I'd have any problem coding this for the 3 dimensional hull... with adjustable parameters like length of midship parallel section, deadrise, bilge radius etc. I would just slice up the hull and repeat the polygonal calc for each slice (station). Dynamics in 3D?...Naaaah...! You have run these OK? http://www.worldwideflood.com/diluvia/beamsea1.exe http://www.worldwideflood.com/diluvia/roll_curve.exe

4. Models... For a QUALITATIVE comparison, I had a thought. I build another 4ft ark model to match my current one - but this time with the pointed bow & stern. Then throw them in a lake or bay and watch what happens. Since visual 'feel' like this is meaningless to a non-observer, maybe I could log some acceleration and bearing measurements of both hulls in the same water and compare them statistically. Ideally, you'd have a collection of hulls with varying degree of taper and determine the optimal seekeeping/volume ratio - but that's too much work. The bearing measurement would be swingable with my students, since we are doing autonomous robotics this semester. If I don't pull off the measurement, at least I'd be convinced if I saw the hull perform better in various wave conditions - and from what I saw of the square ended hull - I expect some improvement would be quite certain.

Tim
